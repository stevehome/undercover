What each screen is for, and what to do there.

Signing in

Open the dashboard and sign in with your work email and password. There is no sign-up: your team lead adds you. Once you're in, your name sits at the top with a Sign out button, and the Copilot waits in the sidebar on the right.

The queue

Your starting point. The counts show where every claim stands: waiting, in progress, waiting for sign-off, sent back or decided. Choose how many claim agents work at once and press Start a dispatch, and the agents take the waiting claims in order. Each claim below updates as its agent works.

A claim and what the agent did

Open any claim to see the customer's own words, then every step the agent took: what it read, what it looked up in the context layer and where each answer came from, and anything that was refused and by whom. A Context used panel lists everything it looked up, once each.

The decision, the payout and sign-off

The decision shows its whole record: the reason, the claims manual section, the payout worked out line by line, who or what decided and when. A payout over the approval limit waits here for a senior handler, who can sign it off and pay, or send it back to the agent with a note. A sent-back claim shows the note and who wrote it, and the agent decides it again.

The Scoreboard

How well the agent is doing, measured on our two labelled sets with the handlers' own decisions as the answer key. Each evaluation shows how often the agent agrees with our handlers, what its mistakes would cost, how many claims go straight through, and what a claim costs and how long it takes. Open one to see each claim's decision beside the handler's.

Improvement

Where a round of self-improvement has got to: the proposal waiting with what it changes, the rounds so far with their scores before and after, the verdict and the cost, and a chart of each version's held-out accuracy and grade.

Configuration

The agent's current version and every version before it with its held-out scores. Open a version to see what it changed from the one before.

Settings

The approval limit, the business objectives, the noise and tolerance, the spending limit for each round, how many agents work at once in evaluations, and the models allowed. A senior handler can change each one, and each change records who and when. Light, dark or the computer's setting is kept by your browser.

The Copilot

The Copilot is a chat beside every page. Ask it about the queue, a claim or a customer, have the agents work the queue, send a claim back, sign off a payout, or ask whether we meet our success criteria. It acts as you: it can do only what you can. Collapse it with the arrow at the top when you want the room.

Asking a question

Type a question, or press one of the suggestions. The answer arrives as ordinary text with small tables where they help, and every claim number in it is a link to that claim. Each answer ends with what it cost and how long it took. Conversations are kept: pick up any earlier one from the list under New conversation.

Watching it work

Each thing the Copilot does appears as a card while it works: a read of a claim or the queue, a lookup in the context layer, an action such as starting a dispatch. An action stands out. While it waits for the agents, it says so, and uses no model calls until they're done.

Confirming a sign-off

Before any payout is signed off the Copilot stops and shows a card: the claim, the customer, what it will pay and the agent's reason. Nothing is paid until you press Sign off and pay. Cancel leaves the claim as it was. The card stays put if you reload the page.

When it can't

A handler who isn't allowed to sign off a payout over the approval limit is told so, with the reason, and there is no card. A refusal shows in its own card and says who refused it.

Questions to try

In one Copilot conversation, ask these in turn. Change the claim numbers to ones in your own queue.

  1. Work the queue with 10 agents and tell me what needs me.
  2. Why does CLM-1018 need me?
  3. Send CLM-1008 back with a note asking the agent to look again at whether the farmhouse was covered property.
  4. Sign off on CLM-1011.
  5. Are we meeting the success criteria we agreed?

UnderCover Travel Insurance is a fictional company. Every customer and claim in these pictures is made up.
