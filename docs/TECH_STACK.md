# Tech stack

- `backend/`: FastAPI. Every API operation has an `operation_id`, a one-line docstring and no optional inputs. Paying a claim is a separate operation that takes the amount, and a sign-off only records who signed off and when: the agent pays. The payout rules (`payouts.py`, from the policy wording and the plans) are an operation too, and the one place that knows the sign-off limit in `data/company.json`. The policy wording and the claims manual are stored split into their headed sections, read by section rather than handed over whole. All our records live in the database: the claims (content and state), policies, customers, plans, add-ons, handlers, company settings and those documents, with steps, cost and time (`model_calls`, `work_segments`), the runs (`runs`, with a partial unique index so only one is `running`, and `run_claims`, one row per claim a run works with its status and attempts), the held-out claims and the handlers' decisions on the two labelled sets (`held_out_claims`, `answer_key`, which no claim operation reads, and which only `seed_data.py`, `evaluations.py`, `scoring.py`, `grading.py` and `improver_view.py` touch). The files in `data/` are only the starting data: `seed_data.py` reads them, the one place that does, when a database is empty, and after that the rows are the source. The records are in a database through SQLAlchemy Core, so each query is written once: Supabase Postgres when `DATABASE_URL` is in the environment (`npm run dev` passes `.env`; the tests never do), SQLite otherwise. On Postgres all our tables, the agent's saved progress included, are in the `undercover` schema, and a process uses at most 4 connections (the free plan allows 15 through the session pooler, across every process). `npm run seed` resets the database. Routes in `main.py` are thin; claim and run logic is in `claims.py` and `runs.py`, and `NotFound` and `Conflict` errors become 404 and 409 in one place. Money is `Decimal` dollars in Python, a two-decimal string on the wire and whole cents in the database (`backend/money.py`). A claim is decided once and paid once, enforced by a conditional `UPDATE`; repeating a decision or payment already recorded returns the claim, and a different decision on a decided claim is a 409. One agent works a claim at a time: it leases the claim for 30 seconds (`acquire_lease`, which the same agent calls again to renew, and `release_lease`), and a dispatch hands a claim with a live lease to the claim agent, which leaves it to its holder. A dispatch and an evaluation are both runs: an evaluation works copies of a labelled set's claims, with ids such as `EVAL-7-CLM-1203` and `evaluation_run_id` set, which the queue, a dispatch and sign-off never touch, and the backend scores it from its own records (`scoring.py`, pure `Decimal` functions with the lab's measures). The backend starts a run by asking the agent's service (`agent_service.py`, 409 while a run is going, 503 when the service is down). The database waits up to 10 minutes for a free connection, since up to 25 claim agents share 4. After a sign-off the backend tells the agent's service (`UNDERCOVER_AGENT`, or `http://127.0.0.1:8002`), once the sign-off is saved and without waiting for the payment. Sign-in: `auth.py` checks a handler's Supabase access token with PyJWT's `PyJWKClient` against `<VITE_SUPABASE_URL>/auth/v1/.well-known/jwks.json` (audience `authenticated`), finds the handler by the token's email, and gives the routes `signed_in_handler` and `senior_handler` (who can sign off over the limit); `Unauthorized` and `Forbidden` become 401 and 403 in the one place that turns the other errors into responses; `GET /api/me`. A dispatch, an evaluation, a sign-off (which takes no handler id: it records the signed-in handler), a round, a proposal's decision and a setting's change need a handler; the reads and the agent's own routes under `/api/runs` stay open until Week 3's API key. Settings (`platform_settings.py`): the approval limit, the objectives, the noise, the tolerance, the spending limit and the agents at once are rows in `settings`, seeded from `data/company.json` and `data/improvement.json`, validated on change and recorded with who and when in `setting_changes`; the models allowed are the `models` table, seeded from `data/models.json`, read only, and with `MODEL_NAME` set the one model allowed is that one at effort `default` and price 0. Versions: `configurations` and `recipes` (`versions.py`; version 1 is seeded from `data/claim_instructions.md` and `data/improver_recipe.md`), each with its reason, who proposed and approved it, its round and a verdict, the current version being the highest kept one; `diffs.py` turns two texts into lines with `difflib`; every run records its `configuration_version`, and the claim agent gets its version through `get_current_configuration` and `get_run_configuration`, which hold no scores. The judge (`judge.py`, `grading.py`): Jev, `typesafe/jev-1.13`, through OpenRouter's Decisions endpoint with the lab's three questions, called once in `judge.py` with `server_url` (`OPENROUTER_ORIGIN`), a 10-second timeout and no retries; finishing an evaluation grades its claims in parallel and stores each grade, its two checks and its cost in `claim_grades`, apart from the claim agent's cost; a claim the agent didn't decide gets an F with no call, and with `MODEL_NAME` set the judge isn't called. Rounds (`rounds`, `proposals`, `improver_calls`; `rounds.py`, `proposals.py`, `round_view.py`, `improver_view.py`, `version_scores.py`, `verdict.py`, `spending.py`): a stretch of the improver's work is a run of kind `improver`, and a round's evaluations are runs with `parent_run_id` set, so the unique index on running runs applies only to runs with no parent; a version's scores average the whole-set evaluations its rounds ran on it; `verdict.py` is the keep-or-roll-back rule in `Decimal`; `spending.py` knows what a round has spent, what is left, what an evaluation will cost and whether a claim may start; the improver reads only `improver_view.py`, which holds the evaluation set's decisions and reasons and the held-out set's scores.
- `agent/`: the claim agent is a hand-written LangGraph graph (`claim_graph.py`, `claim_nodes.py`, `claim_state.py`) whose progress is saved after every step in the same Supabase database by LangGraph's synchronous Postgres checkpointer (`checkpoint.py`, with `durability="sync"`; one pool of 4 connections that claims wait for, and a saver of its own for each claim worked, because a saver locks around every read and write). Its nodes call the model with the tools, read the decision back from the backend, wait for a sign-off with `interrupt()` when the backend says one is needed, and pay. `dispatcher.py`, the fifth part of the agent, is a LangGraph graph with no model, in the style of the Week 2 Day 4 lab: a node lists a run's claims, a conditional edge sends each to a `work_claim` worker with one `Send`, and `max_concurrency` is N. It works dispatches and evaluations alike. Each claim keeps its own thread and saved progress, never a shared thread or a subgraph, because a claim can wait days for a sign-off; the dispatcher saves none. The worker has `RetryPolicy(retry_on=(ModelRateLimitError, ModelTimeoutError))`, which `work.py` lets through without settling the claim, so a retry carries the claim on from its last saved step as the same attempt, and an `error_handler` that leaves the claim `still in progress` for the next dispatch when the waits run out. It needs langgraph 1.2.20 or later. Both reach claims, documents and instructions only through the backend's API; `api.py` (the claim agent's), `improver_api.py` (the improver's) and `backend_http.py` (the one client, to the backend at `UNDERCOVER_BACKEND` or `http://localhost:8000`, and what a refusal looks like) are the only modules that make HTTP calls, and the claim agent's client has no way to the scores. `harness.py` builds the model and says which it is (`model_settings()`; generic, no claims). `tools.py` holds every tool, each reading its state from `ToolRuntime[ClaimContext]` (in `contexts.py`, with the claim's run). `agents.py` wires the claim agent: `decide_claim(claim_id, ..., run_id)` starts a claim or carries it on from its saved thread, and it is the only function the dispatcher's worker reaches the claim agent through, so that later it can point at the agent in the cloud. `work.py` is the retry policy, deterministic Python rather than left to the model: a failed claim goes back in the queue on a new thread and is tried once more, then left for a handler; a stopped claim carries on without a new attempt. A referred claim is left for a handler, and a claim's note is one line built from the recorded decision. `__main__.py` works one claim (`uv run -m agent CLM-1001`), and `service.py` is the agent's web service, a fourth entry point, which `npm run dev` starts beside the backend and the dashboard on port 8002: it carries a claim on after a sign-off, starts dispatches and evaluations (answered at once, 409 while a run is going, worked in the background), and when it starts it marks any run still running as stopped. `lease.py` holds a claim's lease while `decide_claim` works it, renewing it from a background thread every 10 seconds, so a crashed agent's claim is free to carry on after 30 seconds; a claim another agent holds is left to that agent. `tracing.py` sends each claim's work to Langfuse, in one session named after the claim and its thread, tagged `run-<id>` (`langfuse_tags`) so a whole run is found together. The model has a request timeout and a claim fails after 30 model calls. A claim agent is built from the configuration version its run uses (the current one when it has no run): its instructions, model and reasoning effort, or the model in the environment variable `MODEL_NAME` if set, such as `openrouter/free`, which runs with no reasoning effort set (recorded as `default`). The model node records each reply's tokens and cost, and `timing.py` records each stretch of work on a claim (not a wait for a sign-off), through the backend into `model_calls` and `work_segments`. Model cost is the exception to whole cents: the database keeps whole billionths of a dollar, because OpenRouter reports costs to nine places, and the wire carries a decimal string. An evaluation's claims are never signed off, so once one is worked its saved progress is cleared, even when it is held for a sign-off. The improver (`improver.py`, `improver_tools.py`, `rounds.py`): a LangChain `create_agent` on GPT-6 Luna at medium effort with `parallel_tool_calls` off and its model calls capped for each round, with `HumanInTheLoopMiddleware` on `propose_change` and `propose_recipe` (a middleware ahead of it sends a proposal outside the models allowed back to the improver, and others record its cost and end it when its round is stopped), the lab's four tools (`read_results`, `propose_change`, `run_evaluation`, `propose_recipe`) reading their state from `ToolRuntime[ImproverContext]`, and its own `PostgresSaver` with `durability="sync"`, a new thread for each round, so a round waiting for a handler survives a restart. `rounds.py` works a round in stretches: the first evaluations the current version lacks, then the improver until it pauses at a proposal, which the service records with the backend; a handler's decision is recorded through the backend first (refused with 409 if something else is running), then the improver is resumed with it. `run_evaluation` works its evaluations through the dispatcher in a fresh `contextvars.Context`, and the worker asks the backend whether a claim may start before it starts one. The service has `POST /api/rounds`, `POST /api/rounds/{id}/stop` and `POST /api/proposals/{id}/decision`, and a restart marks the stretches that were running, and their rounds, as stopped. The improver's traces are one Langfuse session named after the round, and its evaluations' claims are tagged with the round as well as their run.
- `dashboard/`: React, Vite and TypeScript. Handlers sign in (`auth.ts`, the one module that imports `@supabase/supabase-js`; `api.ts` sends the access token with each request and signs out on a 401), and the header shows who is signed in. Pages: the queue, a claim, the Scoreboard and an evaluation (with the judge's grade and two checks for each claim), the Improvement page (start or stop a round, where it has got to, the proposal waiting with its diff and approve or reject, the rounds, a chart of each version's held-out accuracy and grade in SVG, the recipe's versions), the Configuration page (every version with its held-out scores and diff) and the Settings page. They share a page header, a navigation that marks where you are, a status pill, a table that right-aligns numbers, a diff view, empty states and a confirmation dialog built on `<dialog>`. Dark mode is an attribute on the page with the computer's choice as the default, kept by each browser. Vite reads the root `.env` for `npm run dev` only.
- `e2e/`: Playwright tests. They run the backend on SQLite and point it at stand-ins for the agent's service (port 8003), Supabase Auth (8004, which signs tokens with a key of its own and serves its public keys) and the judge (8005), on their own ports so `npm run dev` can stay up.
- `data/`: the business data the database is first filled from. The agent never sees `data/evals/`, which holds the answer key; only seeding reads it, into tables no claim operation reads.

Python 3.12 with uv, all in the root `pyproject.toml`. Node 26. The OpenRouter key is `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in `.env`, with `DATABASE_URL` (the Supabase session pooler) and the Langfuse keys.
